How to use this page

  1. Edit the prompt here, inside the box. Every prompt is a suggestion. Fill in the brackets and make it yours.
  2. Click "Copy". Your edited version is copied.
  3. Paste it into any free chat: Gemini, ChatGPT, Claude or another.

Answer in English, Arabic or Hebrew - whatever is easiest. Add "reply in English" or "reply in Arabic" if you want.

One rule: nothing the AI says is evidence. Every answer gets a tag:

KNOWN evidence from real people: who, when, how many
ASSUMED our belief, desk research, or anything the AI suggested
UNKNOWN no answer yet - a good answer

Part 1 · Think with AI: let the chat interview you

Two pastes: the master prompt, then one framework under it. One person types, the team answers together.

1The master prompt

It turns a free chat into a skeptical co-founder who asks one question at a time, tags every answer, and won't fill anything in for you.

2Pick one framework for your stage

Open the one that fits the question your team is stuck on, copy it, and paste it under "FRAMEWORK:".

Is the problem worth solving? · 4 U's + 5 Whys (Michael Skok; Toyota)
Who exactly? · Beachhead market + persona (Bill Aulet, Disciplined Entrepreneurship, MIT)
What do they really need? · Value Proposition Canvas (Strategyzer.com)
Which solution? · Opportunity Solution Tree (Teresa Torres)
How does it make money? · Lean Canvas (Ash Maurya)
What could kill us? · Pre-mortem (Gary Klein)
Save your table. When the chat gives you the final table, copy it into a doc or a note. You'll need it in parts 3 and 4. If a free chat runs out of messages, paste the table into a new chat and continue.

3Three habits that make the chat more honest

  • Ask, don't tell: "Is it true that...?" instead of "Students need...".
  • Third person: "A student team proposes..." instead of "my idea".
  • Push back: reply "Are you sure?" If its critique collapses, it was weak.

Part 2 · Research outward

A language model is for ideas and questions. For facts, numbers and competitors, use a tool that shows its sources - and open them.

1Deep Research: a cited market report

An agent reads dozens of sources for 5-10 minutes and writes a report with citations. On a free account, try Gemini: choose Deep Research under Tools. One report uses a big part of your free limit, and Google says it may be unavailable at busy times - so run one, and keep the chat for everything else. ChatGPT gives a limited number of free runs; Perplexity gives one a month.

Before you trust it: open 3 of its sources. Any number without a source is ASSUMED. Then ask it: "What did you NOT find?"

2Gemini Notebook: think across your own materials

Gemini Notebook (formerly NotebookLM) answers from your sources only, with citations. Make one notebook for your market and load: interview notes, competitor websites, papers, a YouTube video, your Deep Research report.

Gemini Notebooknotebooklm.google
Also try: a mind map of your market, or an audio overview to listen to on the bus - it can be made in Arabic too.

3Quick checks with Perplexity

4Patents: has someone already done it?

A first sanity check, 15 minutes. Describe the idea without the secret part, and turn off training in your chat first (see "Your next week" below).

Each patent tool searches in its own way. Instead of guessing the right words, let a chat interview you about the idea, learn how each tool searches best, and write the query for you - one for PQAI, one for Google Patents, one for Perplexity Patents.

Step 2 - search. Paste each query into its tool. On Google Patents, tick "Include non-patent literature". Results look off? Paste what you got back into the chat and ask it to sharpen the query.

PQAIfree AI search Google Patentsfree, many countries, translated Perplexity Patentsplain-language questions WIPO PATENTSCOPEArabic interface EspacenetEuropean Patent Office Israel Patent OfficeIsraeli filings

Step 3 - read the 3 closest. For each: the abstract, claim 1, one drawing, the number, the date, the legal status. Then click "Similar" on the closest one.

Know the limits. This is not legal advice, and finding nothing does not mean the idea is new. Chats invent patent numbers - open every number on Google Patents. File before you publish: Europe and Israel have no general grace period. For free expert help, look for a WIPO innovation support centre (TISC) - for example at An-Najah University, Nablus.

Part 3 · Test before you build

An MVP takes weeks. Your riskiest assumption can be tested this week - without code.

1The assumption map: what to test first

2The test card

Write the number before you run the test - otherwise every result looks like success.

Example: We believe students will buy evening leftovers at half price. We'll put a poster with a sign-up link at the cafeteria. We're right if at least 15% of students who scan it join the waitlist within 3 days.

3Option A · A landing page ("fake door")

A page for a product that doesn't exist yet, with a "join the waitlist" button. First make a short Google Form for the sign-ups, then build the page.

How to share it: in Gemini, build it as an interactive app and export it to Google Sites (it starts as a private draft - publish it). In Claude, publish the artifact and copy the link. Or download the file and send it.
Be honest: the page says "coming soon". Never take money for something that doesn't exist.

4Option B · An interview script, and a rehearsal

Rehearsal, not evidence. AI customers are too positive and too shallow. Nothing they say is KNOWN. Use it to practise asking - then talk to real people.

5Option C · A patent check

The four steps are in Part 2: go to the patent check.

Part 4 · Go outward

A pitch deck, a partner, a post - each one built from your table, so it says only what you know.

1A pitch deck, built in a chat

Any free chat can build a deck as one HTML file: a web page that works like a slideshow. You don't need to read any code.

Where to see it: Claude shows it as an artifact next to the chat; Gemini opens it in Canvas; in ChatGPT, open the preview of the code. All three let you download it. Open the file in a browser and press F11 for full screen.

2Finding partners

Free LinkedIn limits searches and personal notes each month - so make the first message count.

Your next week

1One assumption, one test, one number

2Keep your idea private

Free chats may use your conversations to train their models. Turn it off before you paste anything sensitive:

  • ChatGPT: Settings → Data controls → "Improve the model for everyone" off. Or use a temporary chat.
  • Gemini: Activity → turn off "Keep Activity". Or use a temporary chat.
  • Claude: Settings → Privacy → turn off model training. Or use an incognito chat.
  • Perplexity: Settings → turn off "AI data retention".

4Where the frameworks come from

  • 4 U's - Michael Skok · 5 Whys - Toyota Production System
  • Beachhead market and persona - Bill Aulet, "Disciplined Entrepreneurship"
  • Value Proposition Canvas - Strategyzer.com
  • Opportunity Solution Tree - Teresa Torres, Product Talk
  • Lean Canvas - Ash Maurya
  • Pre-mortem - Gary Klein
  • Assumption mapping and test cards - David J. Bland and Alexander Osterwalder, "Testing Business Ideas"
  • "At least X% of Y will Z" and fake doors - Alberto Savoia, "The Right It"
  • The Mom Test - Rob Fitzpatrick
