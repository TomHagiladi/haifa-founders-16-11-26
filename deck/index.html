The prompt that runs it all

Act as my skeptical co-founder. Interview me to fill in the framework below. Go one block at a time. Ask ONE question, then wait. Tag every answer KNOWN ASSUMED UNKNOWN and say why. Restate my claims as questions. Push back on vague answers. Judge this as a stranger's idea. No praise. Label your own ideas "AI SUGGESTION - unverified". At the end: the table, the 3 assumptions that could kill the idea, and the cheapest check for each.

Short version. The full prompt is on the practice guide - edit it, copy it, paste it.

Tests without code

Don't ask people if they would use it. Make them act - and watch.

Fake door

A "join" button for a product that doesn't exist yet.

Example: a cafeteria poster - "Tonight's leftovers, half price - join" - with a QR code.

You learn: do they want it enough to act?

Mechanical Turk

Looks automatic - but you do it by hand, behind the scenes.

Example: at 18:00 you call 3 cafeterias and post the leftovers in a students' WhatsApp group.

You learn: is the result worth it - before you build the tech?

Pinocchio

A model that doesn't work - people "use" it in real life.

Example: 5 students get a picture of the app screen, and text you each time they'd have opened it.

You learn: would they really use it - and when?

Provincial

The real thing - tiny, and in one place.

Example: one evening, one cafeteria, 10 students. You sell the leftovers yourself, at the till.

You learn: what breaks in real life?

Pretotyping - Alberto Savoia, "The Right It" · Be honest: "coming soon", and never take money for something that doesn't exist.

The Mom Test

Talk about their life, not your idea. Ask about the last time - not about the future.

Don't askAsk
"Would you use an app that sells leftover meals?""When did you last buy lunch on campus? What did you pay?"
"Would you pay 15 shekels for it?""What do you do now when you're hungry at 4 pm and short on money?"
"Is it a good idea?""What have you tried to save money on food? How did it go?"

Let the AI rewrite your questions - it's very good at this.

Rob Fitzpatrick, "The Mom Test"
